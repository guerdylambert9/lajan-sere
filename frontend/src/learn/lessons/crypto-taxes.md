# Crypto taxes in the US

For US taxes, digital assets are property, not currency ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets)). Selling a coin works much like selling a stock: a gain or loss, short-term if you held it a year or less ([Taxes and records](#/learn/taxes-and-records)). This lesson is general information, not tax advice.

## Every sale counts

Each time the crypto book sells, whether by its rule or by a stop, you have a gain or a loss to report, however small the trade. A rule that trades a few times a year per coin still creates a handful of short-term results.

## The form you'll get: 1099-DA

Brokers now report crypto sales on **Form 1099-DA**. For sales in 2025 they must report the gross proceeds, and reporting your cost is optional. From 2026 they must also report the cost of coins they count as covered, generally ones bought from 2026 on in the same account ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099da)).

Your own records still matter. The app's ledger keeps every order, fill and fee, and the reports in `backend/reports/` are yours to keep.

## The wash sale rule, for now

The wash sale rule stops you from deducting a loss when you buy the same security back within 30 days. It typically doesn't apply to crypto, because crypto is taxed as property rather than as a security ([TurboTax](https://ttlc.intuit.com/turbotax-support/en-us/help-article/cryptocurrency/wash-sale-rule-cryptocurrency/L1d6BuQpH_US_en_US)).

Two cautions:

- **Spot crypto ETFs are securities**, so treat the rule as applying to them ([CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/crypto-wash-sale-rule)).
- **It may change.** A bill that cleared the House Ways and Means Committee in September 2026 would apply the rule to crypto sold after September 14, 2026, if it becomes law ([CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/crypto-wash-sale-rule)). Don't plan around the gap; ask a tax professional before you sell at a loss and buy back.
