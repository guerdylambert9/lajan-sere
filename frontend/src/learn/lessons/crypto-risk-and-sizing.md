# Crypto risk and position size

Crypto can lose most of its value, more than once, and quickly. The crypto book's settings start from that fact.

## How far it can fall

- Bitcoin fell about 77% from its November 2021 peak to its low a year later ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/11/11/crypto-bubble-bitcoin-fifth-biggest-all-time-bofa-ftx)).
- It fell again by more than half, from a record of $126,251 in October 2025 to about $60,000 on February 6, 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-06/bitcoin-selloff-explained-what-triggered-the-crypto-selloff-and-what-comes-next)). Ether and other coins fell even further ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/05/bitcoin-price-today-70000-in-focus.html)).

For comparison, the stock rules here treat a 10% fall in the whole account as serious enough to stop new buys ([Drawdowns](#/learn/drawdowns)).

## Why each coin gets about 5%

The stop sits 15% below the buy, wider than the 8% for stocks, because crypto's ordinary daily swings would trip a tight stop all the time. Its limit sits 5% lower still. So in the worst fill the stop allows, a position loses about 19% (0.85 × 0.95 leaves 80.75% of the buy price).

The app's rule doesn't change: one trade may lose at most 1% of the account ([Position sizing](#/learn/position-sizing)). One percent divided by 19.25% is about 5.2%, and the crypto book caps each coin at 5%. On a $100,000 account that's $5,000 of bitcoin and $5,000 of ether at most: 10% of the account in crypto, never more.

## Two coins aren't much diversification

Bitcoin and ether tend to rise and fall together, as they did in 2022 and again in 2025–26. Owning both spreads less risk than owning two unrelated ETFs. That's why the total stays small.

## A stop isn't a guarantee

There's no closing bell, so no overnight gap, but prices can still collapse fast: on February 5, 2026, bitcoin fell 15% in a day ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/05/bitcoin-price-today-70000-in-focus.html)). In a fall like that, a stop-limit sells at its limit or not at all ([How trading crypto differs](#/learn/crypto-trading-differs)). The 1% figure is the plan; a crash can beat it.

## Scams wear crypto clothing

Fraudsters use crypto's popularity to lure people in, often through someone who befriends you online first, and stolen funds can be sent overseas fast, which makes them hard to recover ([Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/crypto-scams)). This app only trades in your own Alpaca account and never sends coins anywhere. Anyone who asks you to send crypto so they can invest it for you is a red flag.
