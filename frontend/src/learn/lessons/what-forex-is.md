# What forex is: pairs, pips and the dollar

**Forex** (foreign exchange, or FX) is trading one currency for another. It's the largest market there is: about $9.6 trillion changed hands each day in April 2025, 28% more than three years earlier ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/press/p250930.htm)). The US dollar was on one side of about 89% of those trades ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/10/dollar-still-dominates-forex-trading-2025-finance-news)).

## Pairs: EUR/USD

Currencies trade in **pairs**. EUR/USD is the price of one euro in US dollars. At 1.0842, a euro costs $1.0842. Buying EUR/USD swaps dollars for euros, so it gains when the euro strengthens against the dollar and loses when the dollar strengthens.

Every pair the forex book trades has the dollar on one side, so every size, stop and loss is counted in dollars, the account's own currency. You pick the pairs: the seven majors, or six emerging-market currencies against the dollar ([Standard or exotic pairs](#/learn/forex-pair-sets)). When the dollar comes first, as in USD/JPY, the price is yen per dollar and a unit is one dollar.

## Pips and units

Currencies move in small steps. A **pip** is 0.0001 for most pairs (0.01 for pairs against the yen), and prices are quoted to a fifth decimal, a tenth of a pip ([IG](https://www.ig.com/uk/forex/fx-need-to-knows/pips-in-forex)). On 10,000 units of EUR/USD, each pip is worth $1 ([CMC Markets](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en-ca/forex/what-are-pips-in-forex)).

Positions are measured in **units** of the first currency. Traders often talk in lots (1,000, 10,000 or 100,000 units), but the app sizes in plain units, as many as the risk rules allow ([Leverage, and why this book uses none](#/learn/forex-leverage-and-sizing)).

## Who's on the other side

Retail forex isn't traded on an exchange. Your dealer is the other side of every trade: when you buy, the dealer sells, and it earns from spreads, fees and your losses ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/CustomerAdvisory_MustKnowForex.html)). That's why the dealer you pick matters. The CFTC lists six registered retail forex dealers, OANDA among them, and suggests checking any firm in NFA's BASIC database before you send money ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/forexfrauds)).

## Why OANDA, not Alpaca

Alpaca, which runs the stock and crypto books, doesn't trade currencies. Its Local Currency Trading lets people abroad buy US stocks in their own currency; it isn't currency trading ([Alpaca](https://alpaca.markets/docs/local-currency-trading-lct)). So the forex book has its own account at OANDA, with a free practice account for paper trading and an API for apps like this one ([OANDA](https://developer.oanda.com/rest-live-v20/development-guide)).
