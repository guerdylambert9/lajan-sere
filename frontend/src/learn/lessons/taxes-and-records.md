# Taxes and records in the US

Selling at a gain or a loss creates a capital gain or loss for your taxes. This lesson is general information, not tax advice; a tax professional can tell you how it applies to you.

## Short-term or long-term

- **Held one year or less:** a short-term gain, taxed as ordinary income ([IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)).
- **Held more than one year:** a long-term gain, taxed at lower rates: 0%, 15% or 20% for most people, depending on income.

The strategies here hold for days to months, so most of their gains will be short-term.

## Losses

Losses offset gains. If your losses are bigger than your gains for the year, up to $3,000 ($1,500 if married filing separately) can reduce your other income, and the rest carries forward to later years ([IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)).

## The wash sale rule

If you sell at a loss and buy the same or a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale, you can't deduct the loss then. It's added to the cost of the new shares instead ([IRS, Schedule D instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1040sd.pdf)).

A trend rule that sells an ETF and buys it back within a few weeks can trigger this. Your broker usually tracks wash sales for you on its tax forms.

## Records

Your broker sends a Form 1099-B each year listing your sales. The app's ledger and the reports in `backend/reports/` are your own record; keep them.
