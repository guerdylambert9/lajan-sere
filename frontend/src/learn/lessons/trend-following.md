# Trend following

**The rule:** own each ETF while its closing price is above its 200-day moving average, in equal shares; sell it when it closes below.

A **moving average** is the average closing price over the last 200 trading days, about ten months. It moves slowly, so it shows the direction of the longer trend.

## Why it can work

Prices tend to keep moving in the same direction for a while, up or down. A trend rule stays in rising markets and steps aside during long declines.

Mebane Faber tested a ten-month version of this rule on US stocks, foreign stocks, bonds, commodities and real estate. He found returns similar to stocks' with volatility and drawdowns closer to bonds' ([Faber, 2007](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=962461)).

## When it struggles

- **Choppy markets.** When prices zigzag around the average, the rule buys and sells again and again and takes many small losses. These are called whipsaws.
- **Sudden drops.** A one-day collapse happens before the rule can react; only the stop and the next close protect you.
- **Fast rebounds.** After a fall, the rule waits for the price to climb back above its average, so it misses the first part of a recovery.

Because of whipsaws, trend rules often win fewer than half their trades. They work when the wins are bigger than the losses.

## Reading its calls

"Closed at $612.40, 6.1% above its 200-day average of $577.10" means the rule wants to own it. A symbol that closes below its average is a Stay out, or a Sell if you hold it.
