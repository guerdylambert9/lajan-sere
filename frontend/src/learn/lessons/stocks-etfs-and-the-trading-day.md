# Stocks, ETFs and the trading day

A **stock** is a small share of one company. An **ETF** (exchange-traded fund) is a basket of many stocks or bonds that trades like a single stock. This app trades ETFs because one ETF spreads your money across hundreds of companies or bonds.

## The ETFs in the strategy

| Symbol | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| SPY | 500 large US companies (the S&P 500) |
| QQQ | 100 large companies listed on the Nasdaq, mostly technology |
| IWM | About 2,000 small US companies (the Russell 2000) |
| EFA | Large companies in developed countries outside the US and Canada |
| EEM | Companies in emerging markets |
| TLT | US Treasury bonds with 20 or more years to maturity |
| IEF | US Treasury bonds with 7 to 10 years to maturity |
| GLD | Gold |
| DBC | Commodities such as oil, metals and crops |
| VNQ | US real estate investment trusts |

The scanner's watchlist starts with the 11 sector ETFs, XLB to XLY. Each holds the S&P 500 companies in one sector, such as technology (XLK) or energy (XLE).

## The trading day

US stock exchanges trade from 9:30 am to 4:00 pm New York time on weekdays, apart from market holidays. The last trade of the day sets the **close**. This app decides from closing prices only, one price per day, called a **daily bar**.

Prices also move before the open and after the close, so the next morning's first trade can be far from last night's close. That jump is a **gap**.

The app never trades in the first or last 15 minutes of the session, the busiest and jumpiest minutes of the day. That's why approved orders go out at 9:50 am.
