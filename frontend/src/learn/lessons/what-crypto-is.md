# What crypto is, and what you'd own

Bitcoin and ether are **crypto assets**: records of who owns what, kept on a public ledger that many computers share. The IRS defines a digital asset as any digital representation of value recorded on a cryptographically secured, distributed ledger ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets)).

## No company behind the price

A share of stock is a slice of a company with sales and profits. A coin has no company, no earnings and no dividends behind it. Its price is whatever buyers and sellers agree on today, which is one reason it swings so much more than a stock index fund ([Crypto risk and position size](#/learn/crypto-risk-and-sizing)).

## Pairs: BTC/USD

Crypto trades in **pairs**. BTC/USD is bitcoin priced in US dollars: buying it swaps dollars for bitcoin. Alpaca lists more than 20 coins across 56 pairs ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/crypto-trading)). The crypto book here starts with just two, BTC/USD and ETH/USD, the largest and most traded.

You can buy a slice of a coin. At $60,000 a bitcoin, a $5,000 position is about 0.083 BTC.

## Who holds the coins

When you buy through a broker, the broker holds the coins for you; that's called **custody**. It's simpler than keeping a wallet of your own, but the safety net is thinner than for stocks. SIPC, which protects the stocks and cash in a brokerage account if the firm fails, doesn't protect most crypto assets ([Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-19)). At Alpaca, crypto runs through a separate company, Alpaca Crypto LLC.

## Another route: spot crypto ETFs

Since January 2024 the SEC has allowed **spot bitcoin ETFs**, funds that hold bitcoin and trade like a stock ([ThinkAdvisor](https://thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/10/sec-approves-bitcoin-spot-etfs-in-milestone-for-digital-assets)). Spot ether ETFs followed in July 2024 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ether-etfs-receive-final-approval-sec)).

An ETF trades only during market hours, in your stock account, under the stock book's rules and gates. That's the route to take if Alpaca's crypto trading isn't open in your state ([How trading crypto differs](#/learn/crypto-trading-differs)).

> Crypto is the riskiest thing this app can trade. These lessons teach; they aren't investment advice.
