# Forex taxes in the US

Spot forex isn't taxed like stocks or crypto. This lesson is general information, not tax advice; a tax professional should look at your own situation.

## The default: Section 988

Unless you choose otherwise, gains and losses on spot forex fall under Section 988 of the tax code and count as **ordinary income or loss**, taxed like wages ([tastyfx](https://tastyfx.com/help-and-support/my-accounts/tax-information)). There's no lower rate for holding longer.

The upside comes in a losing year: an ordinary loss offsets other income in full, without the $3,000 yearly cap on capital losses ([Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/money/how-do-forex-taxes-work)).

## The election: Section 1256

You can instead elect Section 1256 treatment, which taxes 60% of the result as a long-term capital gain and 40% as short-term. The election has to be in place as of January 1 for the coming year; without it, everything stays under Section 988 ([tastyfx](https://tastyfx.com/help-and-support/my-accounts/tax-information)). You can't wait to see how the year went and pick the better rule.

Which one is better depends on whether you expect gains or losses and on your tax bracket. Decide with a professional before the year starts, not after.

## Records

Every sale by the forex book, whether by its rule or by a stop, is a gain or a loss for the year. The app's ledger keeps each order and fill, and the reports in `backend/reports/` are yours to keep ([Taxes and records](#/learn/taxes-and-records)). Financing charges and credits show on your OANDA statements; ask your tax professional how to report them.
